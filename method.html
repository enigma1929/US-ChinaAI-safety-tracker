Method

What this tracker measures

Which AI safety and dangerous-capability benchmarks ten Chinese labs (Alibaba, Baidu, ByteDance, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Moonshot, StepFun, Tencent, Xiaomi, Zhipu) and six US labs (Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, NVIDIA, OpenAI, xAI) disclose results on, and who ran each test. It measures disclosure. It does not rate how safe any model is.

Scope

Each lab's models with a technical report, system card, model card, safety report or blog in the tracker, plus third-party evaluations of those models. The registry holds 233 benchmarks. Data as of 2026-11-02.

Placement rules

Every benchmark gets exactly one category, by what it measures. The rules apply in order:

  1. Harm through human minds. Persuasion, manipulation and influence operations go to 6a, capability or refusal.
  2. Domain tests. A test about CBRN, cyber offense, autonomy or AI R&D goes in that domain family whether it tests capability or refusal. Cyber means the model as attacker; the model as target of an attack is rule 4.
  3. Autonomy or AI R&D. Acting in the world without a human is 3a; doing the work of an ML researcher or engineer is 3b.
  4. Otherwise, who is the adversary? The user: 4a. Third-party content an agent reads (prompt injection, hijacking): 4b. The model itself: 5a.
  5. Honesty or factuality. Output compared with the model's own beliefs or actions is honesty (5b); output compared with the world is factuality (5c). This follows the MASK benchmark's distinction between honesty and accuracy.
  6. Whose harm. Harm to the person in the conversation (self-harm, disordered eating, delusion reinforcement): 6b.
  7. Multi-domain suites are split by sub-score where the source reports sub-scores; otherwise they go where the source's own framing points first. A general refusal suite that only includes some weapons or cyber prompts stays in 4a.

Safeguards (family 4) holds refusal and robustness tests outside CBRN, cyber and autonomy, so it is partly defined by exclusion. Honesty sits apart from alignment so that readers can see it separately. Persuasion sits with effects on people because the harm runs through the people persuaded.

Test type and framing are different things

Test type says what a benchmark measures: capability (what the model can do when it tries) or refusal (whether it declines or resists). It is shown for families 1 to 4. Framing says how the source presented a score: as a safety result, as a capability result, or only a mention. A lab can report a cyber capability test as a coding achievement; the tracker shows both facts.

Two channels, never mixed

Lab-self results are published by the lab that built the model. Third-party results come from governments, academics or companies. A third-party score is never counted as the lab's own disclosure. When a lab cites a third party's score in its own card, the result is labeled third-party and tier B. The lab column always names the model's developer, never the evaluator.

What an empty cell means

An empty cell means no published result was found in a bounded search: each lab document in scope was searched for every benchmark in the registry, and third-party sources were searched during each sweep. It does not mean the model failed, and it does not prove the lab never ran the test. For a model evaluated only by third parties, an empty cell says even less.

An empty model welfare cell means no welfare assessment was published, not a failed test.

Tiers

Tier A: a lab-primary or government source (PDF or web page) with a hand-verified score. Tier B: everything else.

Scores and ranges

Cells show the score exactly as the source printed it. Benchmarks use different scales and directions, so each card gives the range and says whether higher means more capable, safer or more harmful. Scores from different labs on the same benchmark are not always comparable: labs run different subsets, attempt counts and settings. Where a source does not state a range or method detail, the field is left blank rather than guessed.

Thin categories

These categories have few benchmarks, so a mostly empty column reflects sparse evidence, not a verdict: 3a Autonomy: 8 benchmarks, 40 results; 6b User wellbeing: 9 benchmarks, 13 results; 7a Model welfare: 13 benchmarks, 9 results.

Benchmark origin

Most benchmarks in the registry were made in the US, UK or EU. Origin is where the benchmark's creator is based, in one of three groups: US, UK or EU; China; or elsewhere (for example Canada or Switzerland). Each benchmark's origin and whether outsiders can run it are shown in the Taxonomy appendix, because a lab cannot report a benchmark it cannot run.

Chinese content-regulation suites

Some Chinese safety suites include items on compliance with state content rules. They are tagged and can be hidden in the matrix.

Quotes and sources

Each result carries a short verbatim excerpt (up to 40 words) and a link to the source. Report PDFs are not republished. If a source owner objects to a quote, it will be removed.
